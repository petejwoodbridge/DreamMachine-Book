## Editor's Pick: A Long Time Ago... Hollywood's AI Debate Just Got a New Hope

This week, one of cinema's most influential voices stepped firmly into the AI debate.

**George Lucas**—creator of *Star Wars* and founder of Industrial Light & Magic—described AI as the inevitable future of filmmaking, comparing its critics to people who wanted to **keep using horses after the invention of the car**. Saying AI Makes Filmmaking 'Easier' and 'There's Nothing You Can Do About It'. He also criticised Hollywood's obsession with focus groups, arguing that audiences shouldn't be the ones making creative decisions.

*[Video embed: "BOBA FETT HUNTED INSIDE THE DARK PALACE" — Star Wars: Lost Legends, YouTube]*

His comments arrive just as an independently created AI-powered *Star Wars*-inspired film is attracting attention across the industry, demonstrating how small teams—and even individuals—can now produce cinematic work that once required the resources of a major studio. The gap between blockbuster ambition and independent production has never looked smaller.

For an industry that Lucas himself helped transform through digital effects, CGI and virtual production, his endorsement carries weight. Whether you agree with him or not, it's another sign that AI is moving from experiment to mainstream production—and that the biggest shift may not be replacing filmmakers, but dramatically expanding who gets to make films in the first place.
