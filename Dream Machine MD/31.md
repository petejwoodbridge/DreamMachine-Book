## AI Is Everywhere, But Human Creativity Is Becoming More Valuable

The creator economy is entering a new phase. According to Epidemic Sound's Future of the Creator Economy Report 2026, based on a survey of 3,000 professional creators across the UK and US, AI adoption has become almost universal.

A striking 94% of creators now use AI tools, while 72% expect to increase their usage over the next 12 months. AI is primarily being used to accelerate workflows rather than replace creators, with 57% using workflow-focused AI tools and only 28% regularly creating fully generative content.

At the same time, 89% say they feel pressure to use AI simply to keep up with industry expectations. Yet the report's most interesting finding is that widespread AI adoption is increasing the value of human creativity rather than diminishing it.

75% of creators believe human-created content will become a premium product in the AI era, while 83% say human-made sound creates a stronger emotional connection with audiences. Music and sound remain critical differentiators, with 79% saying they directly impact engagement and revenue and 78% believing they help build brand identity.

As AI-generated content floods platforms, creators increasingly see authenticity, distinctive creative voices and genuine audience connection as the factors that will separate valuable content from algorithmic noise.

The report also highlights growing concerns around ownership and trust. 75% of creators believe AI disclosure is essential for audience trust, 73% worry unclear licensing could limit future business opportunities, and 62% have already experienced copyright or licensing issues in the past two years.

As covered by [Musically](https://musically.com/2026/05/29/epidemic-sound-study-examines-creator-attitudes-towards-ai/), creator attitudes towards AI are becoming increasingly nuanced: creators are embracing AI's productivity gains while demanding greater transparency, clearer licensing frameworks and stronger protections around identity, voice and creative work. The message is becoming clear across the creator economy: AI may drive scale, but long-term value will belong to creators who combine new tools with authentic human creativity and strong control over their intellectual property.
