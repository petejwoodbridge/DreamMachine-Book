## Editor's Pick: The AI World Cup — When Sport Becomes a Living Digital System

*Image caption: FIFA World Cup 3d Experience on the BBC*

The 2026 FIFA World Cup is being positioned as the most technologically advanced sporting event ever staged — but the real story is much bigger than football. Across officiating, broadcasting, fan engagement, gaming, security and marketing, **AI is becoming the infrastructure layer powering one of the largest shared cultural moments on the planet.**

Every player entering the tournament is scanned for one second to **create a photorealistic digital twin**, generating 3D avatars that can support **automated offside decisions, immersive replays and new broadcast experiences**. Combined with sensor-enabled footballs, computer vision tracking and real-time rendering, the match is no longer just captured by cameras — it becomes **a live data model that can be analysed, visualised and recreated from almost any perspective**.

Behind the scenes, FIFA is also deploying AI as a competitive tool. A new football intelligence system gives all 48 nations access to advanced tactical analysis, **allowing coaches to query millions of data points around opponents, player performance and match patterns**. The bigger experiment is whether AI can genuinely democratise elite-level insight — giving smaller football nations access to the same analytical capabilities traditionally reserved for the biggest teams.

The biggest shift, however, is happening around the game itself. The World Cup is becoming **a multi-platform storyworld: broadcast feeds, AI-generated highlights, personalised content, Roblox experiences, creator channels and social media communities all built around the same live event.** Fans are no longer simply watching the tournament; they are **remixing, sharing and creating new versions of it in real time**. AI is accelerating that transformation by translating, editing, personalising and distributing content at a scale human teams could never achieve alone.

For the creative industries, the lesson is clear: the future of entertainment will not be a single piece of content distributed everywhere. **It will be a living ecosystem built around a central experience, with AI enabling infinite variations for different audiences, platforms and communities.** The 2026 World Cup is not just a sporting event enhanced by technology — it is a glimpse of how the next generation of global entertainment franchises may be designed.
