## Editor's Pick: ComfyUI MCP — the open source engine behind AI's creative future

Few tools in creative AI have scaled as fast, or mattered as much, as ComfyUI. It began in January 2023 as one developer's open-source project — comfyanonymous, then in the orbit of Stability AI — built to give people real control over diffusion models instead of a single prompt box. Three years on it has crossed **4 million users**, drawn **89,000+ GitHub stars and 60,000+ community-built nodes**, and spun out into a venture-backed company: a $30m round in April at a $500m valuation ($48m raised in total). It has become the "operating layer" where a lot of serious generative image and video work now happens.

My own route in was earlier, and more personal. I first came across ComfyUI through Javi, a developer I was working closely with at the time — and the node-graph thinking under the bonnet wasn't new to us. Back in 2022/23 I was already part of a team experimenting with the same foundational tech (which I take zero credit in, but could totally see the value): litegraph.js, the browser-based node-graph engine ComfyUI's interface is built on. What felt like a niche way of wiring tools together then has quietly become how much of the industry now builds.

And not just in theory — in shipped work. Ad studio **Silverside AI** built SVEDKA's 2026 Super Bowl spot on ComfyUI, billed as the first primarily AI-generated Super Bowl ad. **Series Entertainment** rebuilt its entire game-and-video pipeline on it, scaling past 100,000 assets and shipping Netflix games. Motion studio **Black Math** uses it to turn one-off deliverables into reusable design systems for clients. Comfy Org says Netflix, Apple and Ubisoft are now paying customers. And the model labs have fallen in line: Flux, Stable Diffusion, Alibaba's Wan, Tencent's Hunyuan and ByteDance's Seedance now ship with a ComfyUI workflow as standard documentation — ComfyUI has become the integration target for new models the way Hugging Face became the target for weights.

The reason is flexibility, and creative control.

Where closed platforms hide the process behind a prompt, ComfyUI exposes the whole pipeline as a visual graph of nodes. You can chain models, wire in controls, and turn a lucky one-off into a repeatable production system — the workflow itself becomes an asset a team can version, share and reason about. As foundation models improve, that control layer gets *more* valuable, not less: the bottleneck shifts from "can the model do it?" to "can I direct it precisely, every time?" It's picks-and-shovels economics for the creative economy. As Comfy's CEO put it, "we are not trying to win the next benchmark" — the bet is that whatever model wins, the layer that lets a human steer it is the thing that endures. It is also why the market is bifurcating: closed consumer apps like Midjourney on one side, and on the other a professional layer — VFX houses, ad agencies, game studios — that has consolidated almost entirely on ComfyUI's graph.

The reason it earns the pick *this week*: is that this week ComfyUI launched Comfy MCP in public beta — pitched as the first Model Context Protocol server built for real production pipelines rather than one-off generations. (MCP is the open standard for wiring AI assistants to tools.) Point an agent like Claude, Codex, Cursor or Hermes at it and you can build, edit and run workflows in plain language, search models and nodes, and — crucially — share a workflow URL so a teammate's agent runs the *exact same* pipeline, every generation reproducible. The tagline says it plainly: "turn your agent into a creative technologist." Because it runs on Comfy Cloud there's no local GPU needed; community servers have offered versions of this for a while, but this is the official, production-grade one. The shift is from AI that *makes* content to AI that can *operate* a creative system.

Here's the bigger story, and it's the one for creatives to sit with.

The ideas open platforms like ComfyUI pioneered are now steering the incumbents.... from the model aggregators (Higgsfield, Weavy, Runway) to the big boys. In April, Adobe launched its Firefly AI Assistant — an agent that orchestrates multi-step jobs across Photoshop, Premiere and Illustrator from plain language — and previewed Project Graph, a node-based editor for wiring AI models, Adobe tools and effects into shareable "capsules."

This is important.

Read that again: Adobe is building node graphs and a multi-model roster, and openly treating the connective infrastructure *between* its apps — not any single app — as where the value now sits. That is ComfyUI's thesis, arriving in San Jose. The innovation didn't come from the biggest company in the room. It came from an open ecosystem, and the biggest company is now following and copying it.

Which is why open source matters in the creative economy — not as ideology, but as leverage. **Opening the black box gives creators a say over the tools, the models, and the decisions and consequences baked into the values behind them**: room for models trained differently, transparent development, systems that answer to the communities using them rather than a single vendor's roadmap.

That's the real hook here. ComfyUI isn't just a powerful tool. It's a signal that the next generation of creative studios may be built on open, programmable pipelines — not closed software platforms — and that the people doing the creative work get to keep their hands on the controls.
