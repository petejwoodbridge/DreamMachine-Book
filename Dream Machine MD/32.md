## Editor's Pick: AI Isn't Coming To Hollywood. It's Already There.

The most important story this week isn't Scorsese. It's not Amazon. It's not the first AI-generated feature heading to a major festival. It's all of them.

Across film, television, games and music, AI is quietly (or loudly perhaps) shifting from experimentation to infrastructure. Some creators are embracing it fully. Others are pushing back hard. Lots of people are still hiding their use of it. But the debate itself reveals something important: AI is no longer a future trend. It's already part of the now in creative pipelines.

This week's headlines read like a snapshot of an industry negotiating its future in real time. Scorsese is using AI storyboards. Amazon is funding AI animation while simultaneously facing backlash over it. Netflix is exploring AI-powered discovery. Hasbro is licensing iconic characters for AI voice generation with 'behavioural licensing'. Meanwhile, artists, directors and musicians continue to question what is gained — and what might be lost.

The other interesting signal this week comes from Hasbro, who have rolled out their behavioural licensing model — through partnerships with ElevenLabs — where developers and creators will be able to license iconic characters including Transformers, Peppa Pig and more. This sends a really interesting signal to what is likely to become widespread standards over the coming months and years — as well as exciting new forms of participation, fan engagement with IP and storyworlds.

The technology is improving. The investments are accelerating. People are figuring out how to work within the challenges. The arguments are getting louder, and bifurcated. That usually means one thing: the transition has firmly already begun.
