# Reader paths

*This is a book about creative AI in the ten months between October 2025 and August 2026. It is not a tools guide. It is an argument, with the evidence underneath, about what kind of creative economy is being built right now and what we should do about it.*

You can read this book straight through. It is built to reward that. The book has nineteen sections — a combined Foreword, eighteen chapters, an Epilogue — plus four appendices, sequenced so that each one earns the next.

If you don't have time for that, here are ten ways into the book that will save you from reading something that doesn't serve you yet. Pick one. Come back to the rest later.

### If you are a working creative trying to figure out what to do next

Read the **Foreword**, then **Chapter 2 (A History of Resistance)** for the historical pattern you are inside, then **Chapter 3 (The Human–AI Agency Continuum)**, then **Chapter 11 (What is Newly Possible)** for the new categories of work, then **Chapter 12 (The Orchestrator)**, then **Chapter 15 (The New Jobs)** for the labour-market evidence, then **Chapter 16 (Choosing the Future)** — particularly the section *What working creatives should do on Monday morning.* That is the spine of the *practitioner's* argument. Everything else in the book is evidence supporting it.

### If you run a studio, agency, label or production company

Read the **Foreword**, then **Chapter 7 (The Studios Decide)** — particularly the section *The trap the legacy industries built for themselves* — then **Chapter 8 (The Machine Gets the Money)** for what the capital markets have done to the strategy map, then **Chapter 14 (Coordination Collapse)** — particularly the section *What organisations should do.* Then read **Chapter 10 (AI in Everything, Everywhere, All at Once)** for the platform-economics frame, **Chapter 11 (What is Newly Possible)** for the new business categories, and **Chapter 15 (The New Jobs)** for the labour-market restructuring evidence. **Chapter 16** is the closing argument. **Chapter 17 (The Tools)** is the practical inventory. The deeper organisational-design argument that Chapter 14 rests on — the 2,000-year history of hierarchy as information-routing protocol, the jagged frontier, the vigilance problem, the three competing post-hierarchical models and a 90-day implementation roadmap — sits in DreamLab's published research.

### If you invest in, lend to, or fund creative work

Read **Chapter 8 (The Machine Gets the Money)** first — the Seven-Year Problem, the duration discount, and the four sector verdicts (games, film, television, immersive) read from the allocation data rather than the press releases. Then **Chapter 7 (The Studios Decide)** for the strategy map the capital is pricing, **Chapter 5 (The Slop Ceiling)** for the demand-side evidence underneath the authenticity premium, **Chapter 9 (Worlds, Not Pictures)** for the spatial layer where the discount changes sign, and **Chapter 13 (Authenticity as the New Scarcity)** for the provenance infrastructure the chapter argues is becoming collateral. **Appendix A** holds the underlying numbers.

### If you are working in policy or law

Read the **Foreword**, then **Chapter 6 (The 88%)** and **Chapter 13 (Authenticity as the New Scarcity)** as a pair. **Chapter 8 (The Machine Gets the Money)** for the financing gap the policy instruments have to fill — particularly the section *The map, read whole.* **Chapter 15 (The New Jobs)** for the labour-market policy framework. **Chapter 16** for the four principles. **Appendix A** for the data the policy arguments rest on; **Appendix D** for the comprehensive source archive.

### If you are working in (or covering) the music industry

Read **Chapter 5 (The Slop Ceiling)** first — it's where most of the music-specific analysis lives. Then **Chapter 6 (The 88%)** for the rights-and-licensing argument. Then **Chapter 13 (Authenticity as the New Scarcity)** for where it's heading. **Chapter 17 (The Tools)** for the comprehensive music-AI tool inventory.

### If you are working in (or covering) film, TV or games

Read **Chapter 1 (The Day Sora Landed)** for the watershed scene-setter, **Chapter 7 (The Studios Decide)** for the strategic map of how the industry has positioned itself, **Chapter 8 (The Machine Gets the Money)** for what has happened to the money underneath it, **Chapter 9 (Worlds, Not Pictures)** for what is coming next, **Chapter 12 (The Orchestrator)** for what it means for working roles, and **Chapter 15 (The New Jobs)** for the labour-market data. The harder underlying argument about why the *creative* / *creatives* equivocation collapses under generative abundance, and the storyworld template that Chapter 9 argues is coming for every entertainment franchise — demonstrated live, at six-billion-viewer scale — both sit in DreamLab's published research.

### If you want the empirical case against the AI-doomer policy framing

Read the **Foreword**, then **Chapter 15 (The New Jobs)** for the labour-market reading the book settles on. The long-form rebuttal that underlies it — the Stanford Digital Economy Lab data, Brynjolfsson's worker-level studies, Acemoglu's macroeconomic modelling, and the 230-year track record of identical predictions that have been wrong every time they have been asserted with confidence, together with the adoption-dynamics and shadow-AI evidence base — sits in DreamLab's published research.

### If you are working in (or covering) IP, licensing, brand strategy or legal architecture

Read **Chapter 6 (The 88%)** for the political mandate the licensing-side conversation rests on, then **Chapter 7 (The Studios Decide)** — particularly the new *Position Five: Own the rails* section on Hasbro's Sixth Wall, the CharacterOS governance layer and the corrected record on the Disney–OpenAI deal that never executed — and then **Chapter 13 (Authenticity as the New Scarcity)** for the provenance and disclosure infrastructure that the IP layer sits inside. The long-form treatment of this — the *three Cs* (consent, credit, compensation) framework, the five-licensing-model comparison, the Coase / Williamson / Barney economic foundations, and the conceptual move from *IP as asset* to *IP as environment* — sits in DreamLab's published research.

### If you are working in (or covering) sport, live events or broadcast

Start with the 2026 FIFA World Cup as the largest live deployment of AI in entertainment history: officiating, broadcast, the Roblox second venue, the creator flywheel, the synthetic-rights precedent and the robot dogs — the full case sits in DreamLab's published research. Then **Chapter 9 (Worlds, Not Pictures)** for the storyworld architecture it demonstrates, **Chapter 13 (Authenticity as the New Scarcity)** for why the live, verifiably human moment is where the premium migrates, and **Chapter 7 (The Studios Decide)** for the own-the-rails strategy FIFA is executing.

### If you are reading this in a class, a book club, or as part of training

Read the **Foreword** and **Chapter 1** to get oriented, then **Chapter 4 (Dead Internet, Living Web)** to understand the structural argument, then **Chapter 15 (The New Jobs)** and **Chapter 16 (Choosing the Future)** to see the labour-market story and the four principles the book argues for. The other chapters are evidence and elaboration. The **Glossary** (Appendix B), **Citation Index**, and **Source Index** (Appendix D) are designed to be the back-pocket reference set for the rest of the year.
