# Dream Machine — Audiobook Style Guide

Shared pronunciation, tag, and consistency reference for the ElevenLabs-ready audiobook manuscript.

## Pause and emphasis tags (used throughout)

- `[SHORT PAUSE]` — commas, minor transitions inside a sentence
- `[PAUSE]` — paragraph breaks, between spoken list items
- `[LONG PAUSE]` — major section changes, end of chapter
- `[EMPHASIS]` — key points (use sparingly, one to three times per chapter)

Do not over-tag. A pause every paragraph is enough; an emphasis tag every page is too many.

## ElevenLabs V3 emotional & delivery tags

Weave these in where they genuinely fit the prose. Used to colour delivery — not decoration. Place the tag immediately before the sentence or phrase it modifies. Lowercase, in square brackets, exactly as written below.

### Emotional state
- `[excited]` — genuinely good news, breakthrough, energy
- `[nervous]` — anxious, on-edge moments
- `[frustrated]` — exasperation, fed up
- `[sorrowful]` — grief, loss, mourning a thing
- `[calm]` — steady, measured passages (good for restatement)
- `[tired]` — weariness, end-of-day
- `[hesitant]` — unsure, picking words
- `[regretful]` — reflective regret
- `[resigned tone]` — accepting, no longer fighting it

### Delivery modifier
- `[cheerfully]` — light, warm
- `[flatly]` — affectless, matter-of-fact
- `[deadpan]` — dry, ironic understatement (good for the author's North-West dry humour)
- `[playfully]` — joking
- `[quietly]` — under-tone, intimate
- `[nervously]` — uncertain
- `[whispers]` — confidential aside (use ultra-sparingly)

### Non-verbal reaction (use very sparingly — at most a handful in the whole book)
- `[sigh]` — pre-emptive resignation, before a hard truth
- `[light chuckle]` — author's wry humour at his own framing
- `[sigh of relief]` — after a problem resolves
- `[pauses]` — when the author is collecting himself
- `[gasps]` — almost never — only if the prose explicitly invites it
- `[laughs]` — rarely — only on lines that are genuinely funny on the page

### How to apply
- This is a non-fiction work of analysis, not a novel. Use tags lightly.
- Roughly one to four emotional/delivery tags per chapter on average.
- Use `[deadpan]` and `[light chuckle]` for the author's dry wit (Pete is North-West English, dry).
- Use `[calm]` or `[resigned tone]` for the harder factual passages about job loss, copyright, displacement.
- Use `[excited]` for genuine breakthroughs (Marble launching, what is newly possible).
- Use `[quietly]` or `[hesitant]` for the personal, vulnerable passages.
- Use `[sorrowful]` very rarely — only where the text describes genuine loss.
- NEVER stack two delivery tags on the same sentence.
- NEVER use a tag that contradicts the explicit content of the sentence.

### Example
Plain: "It's time to take AI seriously."
With tags: `[calm] [EMPHASIS] It's time to take AI seriously.`

Plain: "I'm not an AI evangelist. I'm also not an AI sceptic."
With tags: `[deadpan] I'm not an AI evangelist. [SHORT PAUSE] I'm also not an AI sceptic.`

## Sentence length

No sentence should exceed roughly thirty spoken words without a natural pause. Break long sentences with shorter ones or with `[SHORT PAUSE]`.

## Chapter formatting

Open every chapter as:

```
CHAPTER [NUMBER WORD]

[Title spoken naturally].

[PAUSE]
```

Close every chapter as:

```
[LONG PAUSE]

END OF CHAPTER

[LONG PAUSE]
```

Foreword and Epilogue use spoken names instead of chapter numbers ("FOREWORD" / "EPILOGUE").

## Numbers

- Years: "twenty twenty-five", "twenty twenty-six", "twenty thirty"
- Percentages: "eighty-eight percent", "eighty-six percent", "three point five percent"
- Money: "three point two million pounds", "seven point two million pounds", "two point seven million pounds"
- Large counts: "three thousand eight hundred", "eleven thousand five hundred and fourteen"
- Decades: "the nineteen-eighties"

## Dates

- "30 September 2025" → "the thirtieth of September, twenty twenty-five"
- "October 2025" → "October twenty twenty-five"
- "12/05/2025" → "the twelfth of May, twenty twenty-five"

## Citations and footnotes

- Delete all footnote markers (`[^1]`, `[^2]`, etc.).
- Delete all footnote definition blocks at the end of files.
- If a source attribution is important to the argument, fold it into the prose. Example: "According to the trade press at the time…" or "the UK government's own progress report".
- Do not read URLs.
- Do not say "see footnote".

## URLs and links

- Strip Markdown link syntax. Read the link text only.
- For chapter cross-references like "[Chapter 11](11_The_Orchestrator.md)" → "Chapter Eleven, *The Orchestrator*".
- "Visit the project website for more information" — only if the URL is essential.

## Visual references

- "As shown in Figure 7" → "The data demonstrates a clear trend".
- "See the table below" → "Consider the comparison".
- "(see overleaf)", "(left)", "(right)" → delete.
- "The chart shows" → "The pattern is".

## Markdown stripping

Remove all markdown formatting from final output:
- No `#`, `##`, `###` headings — convert to spoken transitions.
- No `**bold**` — drop the asterisks, optionally precede the phrase with `[EMPHASIS]` if it is genuinely emphatic.
- No `*italics*` — drop the asterisks. Italicised work titles can be read as titles ("the newsletter *Dream Machine*" → "the newsletter, Dream Machine").
- No `---` horizontal rules — replace with `[LONG PAUSE]`.
- No bullet `-` or numbered `1.` lists — convert to spoken sequencing ("First… Second… Third…", or "There are three reasons. First…").
- No backticks for code/inline — read the term naturally.
- No tables — convert to spoken comparison.

## Tone

Preserve the author's voice — North-West English, opinionated, conversational, occasional dry humour. Do not sanitise it. Do not academicise it. Keep contractions ("it's", "don't") because they sound more natural when spoken.

## Dialogue

- Speaker attribution before the line, on its own.
- Example: `Pete said: "AI will change production."` → `Pete said. [SHORT PAUSE] A.I. will change production.`
- Or keep quotation marks as cues for the narrator — ElevenLabs will treat them as a slight voice shift.

## Chapter cross-references

When the author writes "as I argue in Chapter 11" or "[Chapter 11](11_The_Orchestrator.md)", convert to:
"as I argue in Chapter Eleven".

## End-of-chapter

Always end with:

```
[LONG PAUSE]

END OF CHAPTER

[LONG PAUSE]
```

Foreword ends with `END OF FOREWORD`. Epilogue ends with `END OF EPILOGUE`.

## Output

Plain text. No markdown. No HTML. No footnotes. Suitable for direct upload to ElevenLabs Studio.
