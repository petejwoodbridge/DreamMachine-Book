Across film, television, games and music, AI is quietly (or loudly 
perhaps) shifting from experimentation to infrastructure. Some creators 
are embracing it fully. Others are pushing back hard. Lots of people are 
still hiding their use of it. But the debate itself reveals something 
important: AI is no longer a future trend. It's fastly becoming part of the 
now in creative pipelines, and new models of licensing with.
This week's headlines read like a snapshot of an industry negotiating its 
future in real time. Scorsese is using AI storyboards. Amazon is funding 
AI animation while simultaneously facing backlash over it. Netflix is 
exploring AI-powered discovery. Hasbro is licensing iconic characters 
for AI voice generation with 'behavioural licensing'. Meanwhile, artists, 
directors and musicians continue to question what is gained—and what 
might be lost, how they need to navigate change, threat and opportunity.
The other interesting signal this week comes from Hasbro, who have 
rolled out their behavioural licensing model - through with partnerships 
with Elevenlabs- where developers and creators will be able to license 
iconic characters...... including transformers, peppa pig and more. This 
sends a really interesting signal to what is likely to become widespread 
standards over the coming months and years- as well as the exciting 
new forms of participation, fan engagement with IP & storyworlds.
The technology is improving. The investments are accelerating. People 
are figuring out how to work within the challenges. The arguments are 
getting louder, and bifurcated. 
That usually means one thing: the transition has firmly already begun.
